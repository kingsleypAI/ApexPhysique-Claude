The Rules
01
Consistency above all
Missing sessions kills progress faster than bad form.
02
Progressive overload
Add weight or reps every single week. You have huge room to grow fast.
03
Rest is productive
Thursday off. 8+ hours sleep. Muscle is built during recovery.
04
Eat to cut & build
2,700 kcal training days · 2,300 rest days · 230g protein daily. 3.5km to gym (warm-up) + 3.5km to work (cool-down) burns ~450 kcal daily. Keep food targets the same — the walks do the extra work.
05
Sleep is non-negotiable
7+ hours. Cortisol from poor sleep directly causes belly and lower back fat storage and retention. The single most underrated factor in stubborn fat loss.
06
Belly & love handles — the truth
You cannot target fat loss to these areas. But the deficit + daily walks + calorie cycling is already the optimal protocol. The oblique twists in leg raises build the muscle underneath. Fat clears from these areas last — typically weeks 8–12.

What To Expect
The scale is a liar. You're doing a recomp — swapping fat for muscle. Some weeks it won't move or will go up. Judge by the mirror, your lifts, and how clothes fit.

Wk 6–8
The visual shift
▼

This is when people start noticing. Shoulders broader, waist tighter, face leaner. The compound interest of consistency starts paying out.

Most visible changeTake progress photos
Wk 6–10
Vascularity starts showing
▼

Veins become visible when two things happen together: body fat drops low enough that veins sit close to the skin, and muscle fullness pushes them outward. Arm veins typically appear around 14–15% body fat — you're close.

At 101kg down from 110kg you're already past the point where most people start seeing it in their forearms and biceps. The pump exercises in your programme (lateral raises, curls, cable fly) are the specific movements that dilate veins and make them more permanent over time.

Three things that directly increase vascularity: stay hydrated (dehydration collapses veins), keep sodium moderate (water retention masks veins), and the progressive overload on your curls and lateral raises — higher rep pump work is the most vascular-specific training.

Stay hydratedHigh rep pump workSodium moderate
Wk 8–12
Belly & stubborn fat shifts
▼

Belly and lower back fat are the last to go for men — this is biology, not failure. The fat cells here have more alpha-2 receptors that actively resist mobilisation. By week 8 the deficit has been consistent long enough to start clearing this layer.

You'll notice it first when relaxed — the belly starts sitting flatter instead of pushing out. Upper abs become visible when relaxed around week 10. Lower belly and hip fat is a week 12+ result.

Three things that accelerate this: 7+ hours sleep (cortisol destroys belly fat progress), no alcohol (suppresses fat oxidation for 36hrs), and your heavy compound lifts (squats/deadlifts spike testosterone and GH for hours after).

Sleep 7+ hrsAvoid alcoholCompounds are key

Perfect Scrambled Eggs on Toast
Post-workout breakfast · 62g protein · high satiety

Ingredients
5 wholeEggs
2 slicesSourdough bread
½Avocado

Steps
1
Put bread in the toaster now — it'll be ready by the time eggs are done.

Crack all 5 eggs into a bowl. Add a pinch of salt and pepper. Beat with a fork for 30 seconds until yolks and whites are fully mixed.
3
Put a non-stick pan on medium-low heat (not high — this is the most common beginner mistake). Add butter or oil.
4
Pour in eggs. Stir slowly and constantly with a spatula, moving the egg from the edges to the centre. Takes about 3–4 minutes.

Oven Baked Chicken & Rice
Meal prep staple · 57g protein per serving · makes 6 portions
35 MIN
▼
Ingredients (6 portions)
1.8kgChicken breasts
900g dryBasmati rice
2 tbspOlive oil
2 tspGarlic powder
2 tspPaprika (smoked or sweet)
1 tspSalt + black pepper
optionalLemon juice (squeeze over after)
Steps
1
Preheat oven to 200°C. While it heats, line a large baking tray with foil (makes washing up easy).
2
Place chicken on the tray. Drizzle with olive oil, then sprinkle garlic powder, paprika, salt, and pepper over both sides. Use your hands to rub it in.
3
Bake for 22–25 minutes. You'll know it's done when you cut the thickest part and there's no pink inside. When in doubt, give it 3 more minutes.
4
While chicken cooks: boil rice. Pour rice into a pot. Cover with water (roughly double the volume of rice). Bring to boil, then reduce heat to low, lid on, cook 12 minutes. Turn off heat, leave lid on 5 more minutes.
5
Let chicken rest 5 minutes, then slice into strips. Divide chicken and rice across 6 meal prep containers. Add steamed veg to each.
6
Store in fridge up to 5 days, or freeze portions 3–6. Reheat in microwave 2–3 minutes.
Seasoning shortcuts: If you don't want to measure spices, just use any pre-mixed "chicken seasoning" from any supermarket. Results in 10 seconds.

Pan-Seared Salmon & Roast Sweet Potato
Recovery dinner · 55g protein · omega-3 rich
30 MIN
▼
Ingredients (1 portion)
250gSalmon fillet (skin on or off)
200gSweet potato (1 medium)
200gSpinach or kale
1 tbspOlive oil
1 tspGarlic powder
pinchSalt + black pepper
optionalLemon wedge to serve
Steps
1
Preheat oven to 200°C. Cut sweet potato into cubes (about 2cm). No need to peel — just scrub it. Toss in ½ tbsp olive oil, salt and pepper. Spread on a baking tray. Roast 25–30 minutes, turning once halfway.
2
When potato has ~8 minutes left: pat salmon dry with kitchen paper. Season both sides with salt, pepper, and garlic powder.
3
Heat remaining oil in a frying pan on medium-high. When oil shimmers (but doesn't smoke), lay salmon in skin-side down if it has skin. Don't touch it for 4 minutes.
4
Flip once and cook 2–3 more minutes. Salmon is done when it flakes easily with a fork and the inside is pale pink (not translucent).
5
While salmon rests: throw spinach into the same hot pan for 1–2 minutes. It wilts fast — just toss it around until it shrinks down.
6
Plate everything together. Squeeze lemon over salmon if you have it.
Don't flip too early: Salmon tells you when it's ready to flip — the flesh will have turned from translucent to opaque about halfway up the side. Flip then, not before.
